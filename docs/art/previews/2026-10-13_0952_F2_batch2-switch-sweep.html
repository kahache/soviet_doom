Soviet Doom — switch sweep (SDSWT1 / SDSWT2), batch 2

F2 · 4 variants × 2 states · proposal, nothing in assets/ yet · 2026-10-13 09:52

Four variants of the switch texture, both states each. One sweep, one verdict: pick a letter (and say if you want a detail changed) and that variant becomes SDSWT1 (off) and SDSWT2 (on) so mod-engineer can declare them in SWITCHES/ANIMDEFS and level-designer can stop using the bunker door as a stand-in on MAP01's three switches. Every variant is 64×128, one wall segment on the 64 grid. Read each card left to right: the two states at 4×, then the switch set into the two walls it will really sit on, then the same thing point-sampled down to 16 px tall — that last strip is the “does it read from across the room” test, and it is where the variants differ most.

The four variants

Measured, not argued. Value is what survives Doom's sector lighting, so the number that matters is how far the switch sits from its wall in luma, and how much the two states differ after being shrunk to 16 px. Note the trap in the palette: the brightest flag red is luma 104 and the green steel wall is luma 101 — a red lever on SDSTEL1 disappears as soon as the sector light drops, which is why the state cue is a cream lamp and the accent is hazard yellow (ART-BIBLE §5, §6.3).

A — SDSWT1 / SDSWT2

Rubilnik: knife switch on a bakelite board The literal object: a cream phenolic board, two brass jaws, a blade with a red grip, pivoted at the bottom. Historically the most correct of the four. Its weakness is measured on the sheet: the lit area is small and the board is light in both states, so the far view changes least.

Reading and recommendation

Recommendation: D. It is the only variant whose identity does not depend on the lamp: the hazard chevrons are painted on in both states, so even a dead switch reads as machinery rather than as wall — which is the actual complaint about the SDDOOR1 stand-in. Its lit bar is wide, so it holds up at 16 px (see the table), and the sliding handle gives a close-up cue as well. B is the strongest far read if you want the loudest possible switch; C is the calmest and the easiest to put next to a poster; A is the most historically literal object and the weakest at distance, and it is the only one where I had to spend red on a handle — which on the green steel wall is worth almost nothing in value.
