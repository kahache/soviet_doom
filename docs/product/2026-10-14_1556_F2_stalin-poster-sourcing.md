# Stalin poster: photograph sourcing, slogan, fit in the fiction (F2)

Date: 2026-10-14. Author: art-director. Russian mirror: written by
`translator-ru` at phase close (D34).
CEO request (2026-10-14): "a Soviet poster **exactly like the one we made
with Lenin**, but with Stalin."

So the method is `SDPOSTR1` v4: a public-domain photograph posterised to
flat palette-v1 inks at 128×256, with a 15-cell sweep for the CEO to point
at, and the same frame and slogan band. Legal criteria are those of
`2026-10-12_2000_F2_pm-soviet-art-copyright.md`. This document holds the
research. Every size below comes from the Commons API (JSON metadata,
2026-10-14). Only candidate A was then downloaded, by the PM with the CEO's
permission (§5); the others were never fetched.

## 1. Candidate photographs

The test, as in the Lenin study: public domain in **Russia**, meaning the
author died before 1952, or the work was anonymous and published before
1943 with the name still unknown 50 years later (Commons, *Copyright rules
by territory/Russia*). It must also be public domain in the **United
States**: published before 1931, **or** public domain in Russia on 1 January
1996 (URAA), **or** a US federal work. Stalin is harder than Lenin. Most good
Stalin portraits date from 1932 to 1950 and are by named Soviet photographers
who lived past 1952: Nappelbaum, Otsup, Shagin, Kovrigin. A single portrait
of him published before 1931 is rare.

**Rejected, with the reason, so they are not proposed again:**
- `Joseph Stalin official portrait.jpg`: Ivan Shagin, who died in 1982.
- `J.V. Stalin TASS Portrait.tif`, 1948: V. Kovrigin, a named photographer. Its US status depends on a contested TASS exemption.
- `Joseph Stalin in 1932 (1)/(2)`: James Abbe, an American who died in 1973, so it is protected in Russia until 2043.
- The 1919 shots at the 8th Congress: Pyotr Otsup, who died in 1963.
- `Joseph Stalin, circa 1942.jpg`: Sovfoto, issued 1950. The author is "presumed" to be a government employee.
- `Stalin ca. 50 Jahre alt.jpg`: CC BY-SA, a film still.
- The `TIME-1930` cover: 400 × 527, and it is a magazine's artwork.
- The 1902 and 1910 mugshots: legally clean, but they show a young man nobody would recognise as Stalin.

**Recommendation: A, the 1930 frontispiece.** It is the only candidate
whose US status stands on its own twice over, not on Russian law. Its
Russian status rests on one fact: **the photographer is unknown**. The
honest residual risk is that a period source names the photographer, and
that it is someone who lived past 1952, such as Nappelbaum. Nobody has
found that so far. A is less dramatic than B, so **B is the fallback**:
better image, weaker paper (its anonymity *and* a publication date of 1945
or earlier, neither documented). C is the choice if the CEO prefers US
certainty over a Russian question. 1313 × 2100 is plenty: the poster uses
128 × 210. Lenin's Zhukov plate is 1432 × 1945.

## 2. Slogan

Measured with this PC's Impact (`--slogans` sheet). The band holds 122 px
inside the border. At Lenin's 19 px «ЛЕНИН ЖИВ» is 89 px, but «СЛАВА
ВЕЛИКОМУ СТАЛИНУ!» is 218 px. So the Stalin slogans are set as a **block of
two or three lines**, same stamp, same hard cut. Short phrases are not
protected by copyright, as the Lenin study established.

| Key | Slogan | Size | Historical reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| `za_rodinu` **(proposed)** | «ЗА РОДИНУ! / ЗА СТАЛИНА!» | 19 px, Lenin's | The Red Army's battle cry of 1941-45, on wartime posters from 1941 (e.g. A. Efimov, Leningrad 1941, National Library of Georgia). Who coined it is undocumented, and veterans disagree on how often it was actually shouted. **It dates the poster to the war**, which is what fits our 1977 setting (§4) |
| `slava` | «СЛАВА ВЕЛИКОМУ / СТАЛИНУ!» | 16 px | The standard cult formula of the late 1930s to 1953 (70th birthday, December 1949). It is the CEO's example. It costs 3 px of letter height against Lenin's |
| `slava_short` | «СЛАВА / СТАЛИНУ!» | 19 px | The same formula, at Lenin's size |
| `spasibo` | «СПАСИБО / ТОВАРИЩУ / СТАЛИНУ!» | 14 px | From «Спасибо товарищу Сталину за наше счастливое детство!», 1936: the caption of the January 1936 photograph of Stalin with Gelya Markizova, then on school banners and posters. Shortened to fit; the smallest of the four |

Recommendation: `za_rodinu`. It is the only one that is Lenin's size,
historically specific and consistent with §4. If the CEO wants his own
example, use `slava`.

## 3. The generator, `tools/art/gen_stalin.py`

This is gen_lenin v4, reused rather than copied. It runs `gen_lenin.portrait`
and `gen_lenin.poster` with this photograph's path, crop and ink count
patched in for each call and always restored (a test enforces this).
`gen_lenin.py` is untouched. What it adds:

- **A crop with the right aspect.** `fit_crop` derives the width from the
  portrait's 128:210 aspect. Lenin's crop was set by eye and comes out about
  13% wide once resized.
- **A multi-line slogan stamp.** It starts at 19 px and shrinks one pixel at
  a time to fit, down to a 12 px floor. The cut is hard, so the band is
  exactly red, cream and gold.
- **The sweep.** Three ink counts (rows 2, 3, 4) by five cut points (columns
  −18 to +18), Lenin's layout. `--slogans` draws the slogan sheet, which
  needs no photograph.
- **The photograph path.** `work/art/reference/stalin_<key>.jpg`, with
  `<key>` one of `1930` (default), `1942`, `1943` or `1929`. If the file is
  absent the script names the Commons page; it never downloads.
- **The crops.** `1930` was set on the downloaded plate: centred on the
  head (x 630-1110, y 50-690 of 1313 × 2100), from the top of the hair to
  the collar points, crop x 528-1213, y 10-1134. That leaves out the second
  man and the hand. The other three are still PROVISIONAL guesses from the
  descriptions.
- **`--preview`.** One sheet for the CEO: the sweep, then the poster at the
  current cell (4C) with each of the four slogans.

Tests: `tests/test_gen_stalin.py` has 28 tests. 25 run with no photograph:
the whole pipeline runs on a synthetic plate in a temp directory. The 3 that
need the real plate skip cleanly without it.

Text-bearing asset (CLAUDE.md, D27): this one is rendered on Windows from
the start, so it is regenerated only on Windows. The lump name is not
claimed here. `SDPOSTR3` looks free in `TEXTURE-INDEX.md`, but naming is a
contract with `level-designer`.

## 4. Design note: Stalin in a 1977 game

This is neutral context, not a blocker. The game is set in 1977, after the
1956 Secret Speech and the 1961 removal of Stalin from the Mausoleum. By then
his portraits were gone from official spaces, offices, stations and the
cosmodrome where Lenin hangs (MAP01). LORE already ratifies "Lenin and Stalin
posters" (Premise), and D12 keeps both as iconography only. The question is
only *where* a Stalin poster hangs without looking like an error. Options,
from most to least natural:

1. **A sealed wartime shelter.** MAP02 is the metro-bunker map, and the
   Moscow metro really was an air-raid shelter in 1941. A civil-defence room
   or command post untouched since the war keeps its «ЗА РОДИНУ! ЗА
   СТАЛИНА!» poster. The slogan dates the room by itself. A good secret-area
   reward.
2. **A storeroom.** The portrait stands with its face half to the wall among
   old stock, a busts-and-banners closet of the kind every institution
   quietly kept after 1961. That suggests a weathered or torn variant later,
   which would be a new proposal.
3. **A private corner,** such as an old officer's locker or a guard's booth.
   NOT VERIFIED: Stalin portraits did circulate privately in the 1970s, but
   the specifics should be checked before anything is written about them.

None of these needs a lore change. The CEO and `level-designer` choose the
placement.
