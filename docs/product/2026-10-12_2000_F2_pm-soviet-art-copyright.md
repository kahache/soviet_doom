# Can we use original Soviet poster art? — copyright research (F2)

Date: 2026-10-12. Author: PM. Russian mirror:
`docs/ru/product/2026-10-12_2000_F2_pm-soviet-art-copyright.md`.
CEO question: "as they are copyright-free, could we use original Soviet art?"

**Short answer: no, not the posters you sent, and not most Soviet poster art
from the era our game is set in. But a large and very usable body of Soviet
material IS free, and it covers what we need.**

## The rule, verified at the source

Soviet works are not public domain by default. Russia protects them for the
**life of the author + 70 years**, and **+74 years** for authors who worked
during or fought in the Great Patriotic War (1941-1945).

In practice a Soviet work is public domain in Russia when:

- the author died **before 1 January 1952**, or
- the author died between 1952 and 1956 **and** did not work during or take
  part in the war.

For the United States the relevant line is the URAA: a Soviet work published
**after 31 December 1930** that was still protected in Russia on
**1 January 1996** had its US copyright restored and is protected there for
95 years from publication. Anything **published before 1931** is public
domain in the US regardless.

So the safe band is roughly: **published before 1931, or author dead before
1952.**

## The three posters the CEO sent

| Poster | Author | Status |
|---|---|---|
| «Ленин жил, Ленин жив, Ленин будет жить!» (1967) | Viktor Semyonovich Ivanov (1909-1968), worked on the TASS Windows 1941-43, so the +4 war extension applies | protected in Russia until **2043**; restored in the US until ~2062. **Off limits** |
| «С именем Ленина!» (space-era, signed) | signed, 1960s-70s | same era, same regime. **Off limits** |
| «ПАРТИЯ — ум, честь и совесть нашей эпохи» | 1970s-80s | **Off limits** |

The *texts* are a different matter: «Ленин жил, Ленин жив, Ленин будет жить!»
is a line by Vladimir Mayakovsky (1893-1930), long public domain, and short
slogans are generally too short to be protected anyway. **We can use the
words; we cannot use their artwork.**

Also free regardless of all this: the **hammer and sickle, the red star and
the state emblem** are state symbols, not protected artworks.

## What we CAN use

1. **The 1917-1930 golden age.** ROSTA windows and civil-war posters: Dmitry
   Moor (d. 1946), El Lissitzky (d. 1941), Gustav Klutsis (d. 1938), Viktor
   Deni (d. 1946), Mayakovsky (d. 1930). Published before 1931, authors dead
   well before 1952: public domain in both Russia and the US. This is the
   constructivist look the art bible already describes.
   Caution: **Alexander Rodchenko died in 1956 and worked during the war, so
   his work is protected until 2030.** Not usable. Verify each artist's death
   date before taking anything.
2. **Lenin photographs published before 1931** whose photographer died before
   1952. Verified example: the **July 1920 portrait by Pavel Zhukov
   (1870-1942)**, public domain in Russia (author died 1942) and in the US
   (published before 1931). It is the single most reproduced image of Lenin
   and the base of countless later posters.
3. **Style itself is not copyrightable.** We may imitate the look of any
   Soviet poster, including the three above, as long as we do not copy the
   image. This is the route the art bible already takes.

## Recommendation for the Lenin poster (`SDPOSTR1`)

The CEO wants something more realistic than the discarded geometric
silhouette. The «ПАРТИЯ» poster he likes is technically a **two-tone
posterisation of a photograph**: red ground, the face reduced to flat
highlight and shadow. We can reproduce that *technique* legally and exactly:

1. Start from the Zhukov 1920 photograph (public domain, verified).
2. Posterise to 2-3 tones from our own palette (cream, flag red, dark red),
   hard edges, no gradients, at poster scale.
3. Add the Cyrillic slogan in our fonts.

The result is a realistic Lenin, recognisably in the style of the posters the
CEO likes, built from material we are entitled to use, and documented in
`docs/art/LICENSES.md` with origin (b): free source with verified licence.

This needs the CEO's go-ahead to download one file, stated in the next
session message.
