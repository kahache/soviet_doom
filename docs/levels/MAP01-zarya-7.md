## 1. Synopsis

The alarm catches Sergeant Vilanova in the barracks of a secret cosmodrome:
he arms himself as he walks, crosses the base his comrades have already lost,
and fights his way through the hangar to the service lift that drops into the
metro tunnels.

## 2. What the map has to do

| Job | How the layout does it |
|---|---|
| Teach the weapons (lore: MAP01 = tutorial) | one weapon per act, each followed by the enemy it is the answer to: ice axe (the shovel until D40) → a single hound; IZh shotgun → recruits at close range; PPSh-41 → the hangar's second wave. The RPG-7 is a secret reward, not a requirement. |
| Teach the vocabulary of the base | the red stripe (`SDCONC2`) is **the route**; the yellow lane (`SDFLR1`) is **the hangar's traffic**, and it points at the exit and at one secret. Both are taught in the first 90 seconds and never lie. |
| "The invasion has just begun" | the base is intact, lit and clean (ART-BIBLE §1: dirt is *use*, not decay). Enemies are few and scattered at the start, massed only in the hangar where they landed. |
| End on the metro | the exit is a service lift going **down**, with the darkest light value of the map (96): MAP02 is already visible as a mood. |

Target length: 5–8 min first run on skill 3; ~3 min once known.

**One gate, two steps, both signposted.**

1. The player enters the hangar at floor level and sees, down the yellow
   lane, the **blast door D4**, shut, framed by the red stripe and a dead
   indicator. The only other red-striped opening at floor level is the fuel
   bay to the north-west. The route is read, not told.
2. **SW-A** (fuel bay) opens D4 for good, lights the indicator, lights the
   control-booth window at the far end of the hangar, and opens the wave-2
   closets. The player now has a lit destination he had not noticed.
3. Behind D4 is a 256-deep **airlock** whose inner door **D5** is powered
   down. The lit booth window is the answer; the stairs to the catwalk are
   next to it, and the PPSh-41 is on the way.
4. **SW-B** (booth) opens D5 permanently. Exit through the airlock.

### Keys and doors

**MAP01 has no keys.** `docs/product/LORE.md` assigns the episode's first key
to MAP02, and Doom's own MAP01/E1M1 are key-free: the first map teaches
shooting and reading a space, not inventory. The key *grammar* ("every key
shows its door before you find it") is still obeyed, transposed to switches:
the locked thing (D4, then D5) is always seen **before** the switch that
opens it, and the switch's effect is visible from where it is thrown.

**Monster closets (v3, §17.3).** Six panels with no use-special of their
own: the three in the fuel bay carry tag 10 and open with D4 when SW-A is
pressed; the three in the hangar walls carry tag 11 and open with D5 when
SW-B is pressed. `Door_Open`, so they stay open; each is a dead end with its
door standing open, so the player who walks in walks out.

Rules that remove whole classes of softlock, applied everywhere:

- **No door ever closes behind the player.** The fuel-bay ambush is wall
  panels opening, never a door shutting.
- **No lifts on the critical path.** Height is changed by stairs only (the
  hangar is reached by a 3-step stair, the catwalk by an 8-step stair), so
  nothing can be left in the wrong position.
- **Every remote door is `Door_Open`** (open and stay) and nothing in the
  map closes it, so no use, repeated or mis-timed, can consume a gate.
  Manual doors and secret panels are `repeatspecial`; since v2 the two gate
  switches are deliberately one-shot, so they stay in their ON picture
  (§16.1).
- **No drop greater than 24 units without a return route in the same zone.**
  The two intentional height changes (hangar −32, sump −96) are both served
  by two-way stairs.
- Monster closets open and stay open; a player who walks into one walks out.

Light policy I fix as owner of the value (ART-BIBLE §4 proposed 112–144
corridors / 128–160 barracks / 160–192 hangar; I keep the bands and pin the
numbers):

- Allowed range **96 … 192**. 96 only in S1, S3, Z10 and the booth before
  SW-A; 192 under the hangar lamps and on the **beacons** that say "go
  here" (the fuel-bay mouth, its window, and SW-A's alcove and D4's recess
  once powered — v2, §16.3). Nothing is pitch dark: the base still has
  power.
- Adjacent sectors that are meant to read as different spaces differ by
  **≥ 16**. A lamp sector is exactly +16 over its room.
- One `SDCEIL1` flat = one 64×64 lamp sector at +16 (ART-BIBLE §4).
- The map gets *brighter* towards the hangar and then *darker* towards the
  exit: the curve of light follows the curve of the fiction.
- The **Light** column of the table above is the plan; the as-built values,
  where v2 pinned them differently, are §16.3.

Texture conventions for the builder (settled by the v1 build, §15):

- **Everything is pegged to the floor.** Doom aligns a wall texture to the
  ceiling, so in any room that is not an exact multiple of 128 the cut panel
  lands on the floor with its grime line floating. `GridMap` therefore sets
  `dontpegbottom` on every line it emits except a door leaf, which has to
  ride up with the door. Consequence: the map contains **no `offsetx` and no
  `offsety` at all** — every alignment in it comes from the 64-grid and from
  that one rule, which is why there is nothing to re-check when a room moves.
- Every door **leaf** is exactly 128 high, because `SDDOOR1` is 128×128 and
  a leaf taller than that tiles into patterned wall. Where the room facing a
  door is taller, the door gets a **recess**: a short sector in front of it
  at the same floor and a ceiling 128 above (D3 in the komnata, D4 in the
  hangar, D5 in the lift room, D6 on the catwalk). D4 is 256 wide = two
  leaves of one `SDDOOR1` each. A test asserts the 128 everywhere.
- A sidedef wears the material of the sector **across** the line, so the
  128-high face under the catwalk and the 64-high flank of the cradle are
  plate while the wall behind them is concrete, and a lintel is made of the
  room it leads into.
- `SDFLR1`'s yellow line is laid in **lanes** (hangar traffic, route to D4,
  the pointer to S3), never as a floor covering (ART-BIBLE §4). A lane is
  **128 wide, not 64**: the flat carries its line on columns 2–8 of a 64-wide
  tile, so 128 gives two parallel lines 64 apart — a marked traffic lane
  rather than a scratch, legible from the far side of the hangar, and
  independent of which way `rotationfloor` turns the flat.

## 7. Secrets

Three, none required, each a *reward for reading the room*, each with a
two-way exit.

How v2 gave them away, and the rule v3 follows (the CEO, walking v2: «he
encontrado la sala secreta (aunque era muy evidente)»): v2's S1 was a plate
on a bare concrete wall in a darker sector, and v2's S3 was pointed at by
the yellow lane, the sign the rest of the map uses for *go here*. A clue in
v3 is **a comparison or a question, never a sign**: three stalls of which
one differs, a slit of light where the wall should be solid, a drain that
has to go somewhere. Each is visible from where the player already is;
none is lit brighter than its room or marked in the route's vocabulary.
§17.5 has the detail.

All three sectors carry the secret flag; all three are reachable on every
skill; none costs health to take.

Two engine facts worth writing down, both learned the hard way:

- A `playeruse` special fires **only from the front side of the line**. A
  door sector therefore needs the special on *both* flanking lines, each
  wound so the door is the BACK side. `LineStyle.back_sector` in
  `tools/sdbuild/udmf.py` does that, and `tests/test_udmf.py` asserts it.
- `Door_Raise` / `Door_Open` raise a door to **(lowest neighbouring ceiling
  − 4)**. The design's sump (floor −96, ceiling 0) opened off a −32 floor
  would have given a 28-unit doorway, i.e. impassable. Fixed in v0 (§14);
  every door's clearance is now checked by a test.

### Three additions to `tools/sdbuild/udmf.py`

Owned by `level-designer` since M2 (§10). All three are tested.

1. **Floor pegging by default.** `GridMap` sets `dontpegbottom` on every
   line it emits, except where a `LineStyle` opts out — which the door
   styles do, because a leaf is an upper texture that must ride up with the
   door's ceiling. Result: panels stack from the ground, the cut hides at
   the ceiling, and the red stripe is at the same height in every room.
   **The map contains no texture offsets at all.**
2. **Per-sector wall material** (`GridMap.set_wall`). A one-sided wall wears
   its own room's material; a two-sided line wears the material of the
   sector **across** it, because what it draws is that sector's structure.
   The first v1 build got this backwards and the transporter cradle came out
   as a concrete plinth in a steel hangar.
3. **`LineStyle.texture` used for single wall runs**, not only for doors:
   that is how the red route is painted onto four walls of otherwise plain
   concrete without giving those rooms a material of their own.

everywhere (tested). The range is still 96…192 (tested).

**How the light tag works, and why it is used.** One line carries one
special, and SW-A's has to open D4. `Door_Open`'s third argument is a
*light tag* (argument shape from `gzdoom.pk3 → xlat/base.txt`: Doom's line
31 is `Door_Open (0, D_SLOW, tag)`). As the door rises, every sector with
that tag is moved towards the light of its **brightest neighbour**, reaching
it when the door is fully open (Boom's partial-light-on rule; in GZDoom,
`EV_LightTurnOnPartway`, called from the door thinker — from memory of the
source, **NOT VERIFIED**). The map is built for that rule: D4's own sector
is 192, so its recess ends at 192; the booth's brightest neighbour is its
window, 160; the alcove's is the window strip, 192. A test applies the
rule to the plan. If the engine does not behave like this, nothing
mechanical breaks — the doors open regardless — but the three light
changes do not happen, and checklist E4–E6 will show it.

`Light_ChangeToValue` (110) and `Light_Flicker` (113) are **not used**. Each
needs a line of its own to fire it: the first would need a second switch
or a walk-over trigger (which would light the booth before SW-A), and the
second would only start flickering when something crossed a line. The
flicker in Z5 is ambient from the first tic, which is what a **sector**
special is for: `dLight_Flicker`, value **65** in the zdoom namespace (the
number is read from `gzdoom.pk3 → xlat/defines.i`, where Doom's sector type
1 is translated into it; random flicker between the sector's own light and
its darkest neighbour's). Behaviour NOT VERIFIED.
